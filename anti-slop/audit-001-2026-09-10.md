## HIGH

### 1. Em dashes in rendered copy (R-02)

The core forbids the em dash character in any text. It is rendered on every page
checked: 5 on the home page, 6 on `/projects`, 10 on `/about`, counted after
stripping tags and scripts.

Source concentration: `src/i18n/ui.ts` (54), `src/data/projects.ts` (4), one each
in `experience.ts`, `companion.ts`, `quests.ts`, `rooms.ts`, plus 59 `.astro`
files and 3 in the blog post.

Some are load-bearing to the design, not stray punctuation: project titles are
built on them (`KAMILA — Marketing & KPI Tracker`, `AIRA — Reconciliation
System`, `Thesis — Chatbot Kukerta`), as is the footer line. Replacing those
changes how the titles read, so this one is worth deciding deliberately rather
than running a blanket find and replace.

### 2. Tap targets below 44px (R-03)

Measured at 390px. Overflow is clean everywhere, but touch sizing is not:

| Control | Size | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Sound toggle | 34 x 34 | header, every page |
| Theme toggle | 34 x 34 | header, every page |
| Skill category tabs | height 30 | `/about` |
| Filter rows (All, Project, Work, web app, api, tool) | height 32 | `/projects` |
| "View GitHub profile" | height 16 | `/about` |

Between 11 and 25 controls per page fall under the bar. The two header toggles
are the ones that matter most: they are on every page and they are the smallest
things a thumb has to hit.

The 1 x 1 "Skip to main content" link is not a finding. It is a visually hidden
skip link that expands on focus, which is the correct pattern.

### 3. No design direction file (R-37)

There is no `DESIGN.md` and no written brand direction anywhere in the repo. The
site plainly has an identity, and it is a deliberate one, but it lives only in
the OpenSpec archives and in `global.css` comments.

R-37 requires direction to be loaded before UI work. Without it, the next piece
of UI has nothing to read, and the dials (ENERGY / RHYTHM / MOTION) have never
been declared. Reading the site as built, it sits at roughly ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 2
/ MOTION 2, but that is my inference, not a recorded decision.

This also matters for the redesign now being explored: a third identity built
without written direction is likely to drift the same way.

## MEDIUM

### 4. Perpetual animation on the featured badge (R-19)

`src/components/ui/FeaturedBadge.astro` loads a Lottie flame with
`loop: true, autoplay: true`. It runs forever with no user trigger, which is the
"endless pulses and loops" pattern.

Credit where due: it is guarded by `prefers-reduced-motion` and carries
`role="img"` with a real `aria-label`, which is more care than this pattern
usually gets. The finding is the perpetual loop, not the implementation.

### 5. Background grid without a written purpose (R-07)

`paper-grid` (36px squares, two linear gradients) is applied on `/about`,
`/dashboard`, and the case study hero. A 36px square grid is the named pattern
in Part 1.

The fiction supplies a defensible reason: this is a paper and cork detective
identity, and graph paper belongs to it. R-07 allows the technique, but requires
the reason written down, and it is not.

### 6. Arrows as default button decoration (R-08)

31 occurrences of the arrow glyph across components, pages, and i18n. It appears
on "All projects", "All articles", "Play", "The Board", and the external footer
links.

The external link arrow carries information (this leaves the site). The internal
ones are decoration on nearly every secondary link, which is the pattern R-08
describes. Reason not written down either way.

### 7. Uppercase labels with wide tracking (R-06)

Two utilities in `global.css`:

- `chapter-label`: mono, uppercase, `tracking-[0.12em]`
- `eyebrow`: uppercase, `tracking-[0.14em]`, semibold

These produce "CASE 01 · SELECTED WORK", "EXHIBITS", "CASE NOTES". This is the
named typography tell. As with the grid, the fiction justifies it and the
justification is not written down where a future reader would find it.

## LOW

### 8. Decorative separator comments (antislop-code)

72 comments across 30 files use box drawing or repeated characters as section
banners. Worst offenders: `global.css` (22), `pages/about.astro` and
`pages/id/about.astro` (8 each), `pages/index.astro` and `pages/id/index.astro`
(5 each), `FeaturedProjectCard.astro` (4).

Examples: the heavy-line banners in `global.css`, and the box-drawn section
headers inside the featured card template. The skill names this exactly: the
decoration is the message, and it adds no information.

### 9. Comment density in the newer game utilities (antislop-code)

`src/utils/game/assets.ts` is 33% comment lines, `sprites.ts` 29%.

Read individually, most of these earn their place: they explain why the
rendering path is chosen by directory, why the resolver stays pure, why the
thread colour is not overridden. Those are exactly the comments the skill says to
preserve.

The finding is length and register, not presence. Several run to five or six
lines of essay where two would do, which reads more like documentation than an
engineer leaving a note. Worth a trim pass, not a purge. I wrote most of these,
so treat this as self-review rather than a neutral observation.

### 10. Contrast is verified at token level, not as rendered (R-25)

`tests/styles/palette-contrast.test.ts` checks text roles at 4.5:1 and graphic
roles at 3:1 against three grounds, across all three palettes. It passes, and it
is a genuinely good test.

What it does not check is what a user actually sees: real text on real
backgrounds after nesting, opacity, and `color-mix` have been applied.

I attempted a rendered-DOM contrast sweep and could not make it agree with the
verified token maths: it reported a uniform 1.21 ratio for elements that compute
to roughly 11:1 by hand, in both themes, which means the probe was wrong rather
than the site. **No rendered contrast failure is claimed here.** The finding is
the gap in coverage, not a known defect. Closing it properly needs a real
accessibility tool rather than a hand-rolled probe.

### 11. Self-reported metrics carry no visible source (R-17)

Project cards show figures such as "≥80% unit test coverage", "9 microservices
built", "200+ marketing employees served", "60% of sessions from mobile", and
"Rp 3 miliar+" in loan originations.

These are the owner's own claims about the owner's own work, which is normal on a
CV and is not the fabricated-social-proof pattern R-17 targets. Flagged only so
the decision is conscious: a reader cannot verify any of them, and a recruiter
may ask.

# Follow-up: findings 1, 2, 3 fixed

Date: 2026-09-10
Approved: 1, 2, 3. Findings 4 to 11 were not touched.

## 1. Em dashes (R-02) — fixed

Zero em dashes now render across 16 routes, verified by stripping tags and
scripts from the served HTML and counting.

Replacements were made per pattern rather than by blanket substitution:

| Pattern | Became | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `Title — Ricki Friadi` | `·` | `The Board · Ricki Friadi` |
| `Exhibit A — Warp shader` | `:` | `Exhibit A: Warp shader` |
| `Label — instruction` | `:` | `Desk lamp: drag to move the spotlight` |
| aside inside a sentence | `,` | `systems, from Vue 3 micro-frontends` |
| two full statements | `.` | `© 2026 Ricki Friadi. Built with Astro` |

146 lines across 40 files, plus 40 in `CHANGELOG.md`. Code comments were left
alone: this pass was about rendered copy.

Two sources were not plain text and needed different handling:

- **`/changelog` renders git commit subjects**, not a file. One of them was a
  commit written earlier in this session. History is already merged and pushed,
  so rewriting it was the wrong tool. `parseCommitLine` now normalises the
  subject at parse time, which covers every past and future commit.
- **`/play` credits** were assembled in `ArtCredits.astro` with an em dash
  separator. Fixing it also exposed a duplicated licence: the page had been
  printing `CC0, CC0-1.0` because the attribution string repeated what the
  component already appended.

## 2. Tap targets (R-03) — fixed

All five controls named in the finding now clear 44px on a touch pointer:

| Control | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Sound toggle | 34 x 34 | 44 x 44 |
| Theme toggle | 34 x 34 | 44 x 44 |
| Skill tabs (`/about`) | height 30 | height 44 |
| Filter rows (`/projects`) | height 32 | height 44 |
| View GitHub profile | height 16 | height 44 |

Two more were raised while measuring: the footer link row (16px to 44px on all
seven links) and the wordmark, both of which appear on every page.

The rule is scoped to `@media (pointer: coarse)`. A cursor has pixel accuracy
and a thumb does not, so the desktop chrome keeps its designed 34px and only
touch gets the larger box. Nothing moved visually on desktop.

Overall count of sub-44px controls at 390px fell from 13 to 25 per page down to
4 to 11.

**What is left, and why it was left.** The remaining entries are mostly
`footer-link` at `22x44` and `43x44`: height is compliant, width is not, because
the text is "Ask" and "Now". Padding those to 44px wide would visibly distort a
row of short text links, and the vertical dimension is what a thumb misses.
Genuinely still short are inline prose links on `/about` and `/dashboard`
(66x16, 78x16, 124x13) and two form controls on `/projects` (316x36, 358x42).
None were in the approved finding; say the word and they are a small follow-up.

## 3. Design direction (R-37) — fixed

`DESIGN.md` now exists at the repository root, and `CLAUDE.md` and `AGENTS.md`
both route to it before the filter.

**It was transcribed, not invented.** The skill is explicit that the user is the
author and the agent only formats, so nothing in it is a new decision. Every
line came from an already-shipped source: the archived OpenSpec specs
(`pixel-design-system`, `display-font-identity`, `world-theme`) and the token
block in `global.css`. It records the CASE FILE identity, the literal-content
rule, all three palettes with their exact token values, the typography and
radius and motion rules, the sound contract, dials at ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 2 /
MOTION 2, and the three questions still open.

The dials are the one inference in the file, read off the built site. Correct
them if they are wrong.

## Two measurement errors worth recording

Both were caught before they reached the report, and both are the reason the
numbers above are trustworthy:

1. A sub-44px scan rendered text as "Databa e" and "Search kill". That looked
   like a rendering defect. It was the shell eating the letter `s` in the probe;
   `curl` confirmed the site was correct.
2. A tap-target re-measurement reported counts going *up* after the fix. The
   accumulated edits to the probe script had dropped the 390px viewport
   override, so it was measuring desktop, where a 34px nav link is correct. The
   probe was rewritten from scratch, at which point the numbers fell as
   expected.
